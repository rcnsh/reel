# Hairlines keep their 1x ink at higher output scales

Shaders draw hairlines with the idiom `1 - smoothstep(a, b, d)`, where `d` is in physical px (`dist / fwidth`) or `a`, `b` are multiples of `fwidth`, and the thresholds were tuned in 1x device pixels. At output scale 2 or more, `fwidth` is a smaller footprint, so such a line would come out thinner and lighter than in the 1x look.

The helpers in `src/engine/glsl/common.ts` keep the line's ink (its mean coverage) at the 1x value and draw it with the sharper physical footprint. `_ss3` is the integral of smoothstep's cubic; `_inkHalfWidth(L, R, X)` is the ink half-width that `1 - smoothstep(L, R, x)` puts down over `0 <= x <= X`; `_boxLine(x, m, r)` is a line of half-width `m` box-filtered over ±r, whose ink is exactly `m`. `pxLine(d, a, b)` is identical to the idiom at scale 1; above it the line keeps its 1x ink (`PX_SCALE` times wider in physical px) with an edge ramp of `(b - a)` physical px: the same look, sharper. `rampLine(d, hw, aa)` does the same for the linear-ramp idiom `sat((hw - d) / aa + 0.5)` with `aa = k * fwidth`. `hatchD` keeps the ink the 1x footprint puts down per period and draws it with the physical footprint, and `aaStroke` likewise. At scale 1 each helper compiles to the plain idiom, so 1x renders are unchanged.
