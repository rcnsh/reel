# Engine internals

How `src/engine/` and the page (`src/main.ts`) work, for changing them. Writing scenes and configs is in the `reel-authoring` skill (`skills/reel-authoring/`); the render-and-check loop in `reel-review-loop`. The engine is adapted from the upstream engine of [mexicat/pdoom-video](https://github.com/mexicat/pdoom-video) (MIT, Giacomo Magnanini; see `LICENSE`).

## The page

`cli/server.ts` serves `index.html` and `src/main.ts` with Vite, the project mapped in as `@project/` (its config) and its files served from the site root. Two things are compile-time constants (Vite `define`), so they are in place before any module runs: `__REEL_FORMAT__` (size, fps) and `__REEL_THEME__` (the theme the CLI resolved from the config with `src/themes.ts`). The page URL overrides the size and rate (`?w=&h=&fps=`), sets the output scale (`?scale=`) and can name a built-in theme (`?theme=`, what `--theme` sends); `format.ts` and `palette.ts` read them at import. Because the palette is fixed at import, `glsl/common.ts` bakes every key into `GLSL_COMMON` as `C_<KEY>` and `post.ts` its defaults; a change to the config's format or theme needs a new dev server, which the warm renderer handles by restarting Vite.

`main.ts` boots the `Engine` and then either the preview player or, with `?export=1`, the export API `window.__reel`: `still`, `png` (the readback as PNG, optionally cropped, downscaled and with rectangles marked), `grid` (sheets and strips), `inspect` (`reel check`), `stream` (video frames), `clipsAt`, `timeline`, `cues`, `errors`, `beginRequest`, `contextLost`. The CLI's ops (`cli/ops.ts`) are thin drivers of that API.

## Engine.init

Loads the words, the audio analysis, the fonts (the Canvas2D instances, the families HTML layers name, and the project's `fonts/` listed by the dev server at `/@reel/fonts.json`, described from the files with fontkit in `cli/fonts.ts`) and the stroke fonts in parallel, calls the config's `timeline()`, checks the clips (unique ids, windows, `check.exempt` rules), sets the duration (config, else audio, else the last clip's end), appends the theme's overlay as the clip `overlay` on a track above every other, allocates per track three scene targets and two transition mixes, and loads each clip's scene: the modules import in parallel, then each scene is constructed and initialised in turn with `htmlLayers.owner(id)` set, so the HTML layers it makes belong to its clip; then the loads components registered with `awaitBeforeFirstFrame` (images) are awaited. An `ImageBox` decodes through `createImageBitmap` with colour conversion (an `<img>` uploaded by three keeps a P3 file's numbers, since three asks for no conversion of sRGB textures), a 16-bit PNG through `src/engine/png16.ts` into a half-float texture of linear values (every browser decode path, the float16 canvas and ImageDecoder included, stops at 8 bits), and an SVG drawn into a canvas at its on-screen size. A `VideoClip` (`src/kit/video.ts`) reads the MP4's sample table with mp4box (presentation times from the first shown frame, so an edit list's B-frame delay drops out) and decodes with a fresh `VideoDecoder` per group of pictures, from its keyframe to the last frame wanted, preferring the software decoder; output frames go straight into `SRGB8_ALPHA8` textures with mipmaps (`texSubImage2D` from the `VideoFrame`, the browser converting YUV) and are closed. Its `prepare` picks the frame at the output frame's own time (`Frame.exposure` tells `draw` which sub-frames belong to it), decodes the frames it needs plus up to half the cache's worth after them for a playing export, and drops the least recently used past the cache. A component made during a scene's construction reads the clip being loaded from `LOADING.clip` (`scene.ts`): the video places its sound in video time with `sound()`. A `LottieBox` loads lottie-web's light canvas player on demand and calls `goToAndStop` with a fractional frame per sub-frame, uploading the canvas when the frame changed. A clip whose scene fails to load is recorded in `loadErrors` and drawn dark red. `--only` loads the selected clips plus the overlay.

## Prepare: once per output frame

`Engine.prepare(t, dt, samples, shutter)` (async) runs before a frame's sub-frames; `renderFrame` is prepare then render, and every path of the page (stills, sheets, check, the video stream, perf, the preview loop) goes through it. For each loaded clip on screen during the frame's shutter it calls the scene's `prepare(f)` (f clamped into the clip's window), then `htmlLayers.prepare` (`html.ts`): layers of those clips are shown and synced (their `update(f)`, every animation of the shadow root paused and set to `f.lt` less the element's `data-at`, transitions finished), the others hidden (`display: none`). A layer is dirty when its state string (shadow markup, host style, each animation's computed progress) differs from what it last uploaded. Dirty layers: images decoded, one `paint` event awaited on the canvas (`requestPaint()`), then `texElementSubImage2D` into the layer's RGBA8 texture, premultiplied (`UNPACK_PREMULTIPLY_ALPHA_WEBGL`), wrapped as a three.js `ExternalTexture`. The unpack flags go through three's state cache (`renderer.state.pixelStorei`): set on the context directly, three would skip resetting `UNPACK_FLIP_Y_WEBGL` for the next `Layer2D` upload and that canvas would come out upside down. Chrome runs with `--disable-frame-rate-limit`, so a paint comes as soon as it is asked for (about 0.2 ms) rather than on the next 60 Hz tick.

Layers are `drawable` children of the engine's canvas (`content="drawable"`). They rasterise at the canvas's backing size over its layout size, which is why the canvas is laid out at exactly W x H in every mode and the preview fits it to the window with a CSS transform. `draw()` composites through an FSPass that maps each output pixel back into the layer through the inverse of the draw transform, unpremultiplies, decodes sRGB and premultiplies again (filtering premultiplied texels keeps moving edges free of dark fringes). Errors in a layer (a font that is not loaded, an image that does not load, an `update` that throws) are render errors of its clip.

## A frame

`render(t, dt, toScreen, samples, shutter)`:

1. `composite(t)`: the clips active at t, grouped by track in number order. Per track, `compositeTrack` renders each clip into a scene target (stateful scenes are reset and pre-rolled at the frame step after a seek), draws overlapping clips' transitions with `TransitionPass` (`transitions.ts`; the incoming clip's `transition`, a crossfade by default) unless the incoming scene `handlesTransition`, and merges post overrides (clip `post`, then the scene's return). The lowest track is used as is when it is opaque, unblended and alone; otherwise tracks stack into `stackRT` with their blend and opacity (an opacity fades from clip to clip across a transition).
2. Motion blur (`samples` > 1): sub-frames summed into `sumRT`, averaged into `avgRT`. Post parameters come from the sub-frame nearest 1/8 of the shutter after t.
3. `post.render` (bloom pyramid, halation, CA, shoulder, invert, flash, vignette, grain, dither, sRGB) into `finalRT`, then blitted to the canvas.

A scene that throws in `render()` is recorded once per clip in `renderErrors` (cleared by `clearRenderErrors`, which the warm renderer calls per request through `beginRequest`) and drawn dark red. A shader that fails to compile surfaces through three's `debug.onShaderError`, recorded in `loadErrors` (three compiles a program once) with the drawing clip's id and the source line each error points at.

## Adaptive sampling

`--samples auto` (`AdaptiveSampling`) steps the sub-frame count through 4, 12, 36, 108, 324. `ternaryOffsets` orders the shutter offsets so that each step adds a sub-frame either side of every existing one: every prefix of 4·3^l is evenly spread and centred on t, and so is each step's new set, so comparing the new set's average with the old one's measures sampling error rather than a shift in time. After each step, `sampleError` renders the displayed difference (through the tone shoulder and sRGB, worst channel, 2x2-logical-px blocks, `errRT`) and reduces it to a max per 32x32-logical-px tile (`maxRT`, read back). Stepped copies of a moving edge shrink as 1/count, so the error left is about half the last step's change.

Per tile (`refine: 'tiles'`, the default): a tile goes on while it or one of its 8 neighbours is at or over `tol`; a tile that stops is final, the average of its first n sub-frames. Later sub-frames skip stopped tiles: `writeTileMask` writes the live tiles into the depth buffer of every masked target (the engine's, plus scenes' `tileMasked` targets, `moving` ones through `mapTileMask`; while a slide or zoom transition falls in the frame's shutter, `displacedAt` leaves every track's scene targets unmasked, since the transition reads them away from the pixel they land on), and while `masking` is on, `hookDepthState` forces every draw into a masked target to depth-test LEQUAL against it without writing depth, whatever its material says, so the GPU rejects stopped tiles before shading (early-Z; a stencil test does not reject early on Apple GPUs). A draw whose material wants its own depth test sets `depthSeen`; that clip's sub-frames are then drawn unmasked from then on (`depthEntries`). The final average weights each tile by its own count (`gainPass`). `refine: 'frame'` decides for the whole frame at once.

`SUB_FRAME.index` (`gl.ts`) is the sub-frame being drawn, in sampling order (-1 in a render of one sample), set by `sub()` beside `SS_TAP`. Jitter that should average out over the sub-frames is indexed by it with low-discrepancy sequences (`src/three/jitter.ts`: R2 for sub-pixel offsets, Halton bases 2 and 3 for the lens, 5 and 7 for area lights), so every prefix of 4·3^l sub-frames, where a tile may stop, holds an evenly spread share of the jitter; the sequences are irrational or of bases other than 3, so they do not line up with the ternary order of the shutter times.

A scene's own depth-tested 3D cannot draw into a masked target (the mask owns its depth buffer), so it draws into a target of its own and composites that into `out`. `ctx.maskDepth(rt)` clears that target's depth and, in a masked sub-frame, draws `nearPass` into it: depth 0, the near plane, over the tiles that have converged, so the 3D's own depth test rejects them before shading while live tiles depth-test normally. The composite into `out` is then masked like any draw. `Stage3D` (`src/three/stage.ts`) does this; what masking cannot save is per-sub-frame work outside fragment shading (shadow maps, vertices).

In practice a still frame stops at 12, ordinary camera motion at 36, whips and fast zooms at 108 or 324; at 1:1 in 4K, 108 cannot be told from 324.

## The 3D stage

`src/three/stage.ts` renders a three.js scene into its own HalfFloat target (`makeRT`), or a 4x MSAA one when `SUB_FRAME.index` is -1, then composites it into the scene's `out`. Per sub-frame it shifts the projection matrix's third column for a sub-pixel offset (`subPixel(k)`) and, for depth of field, translates the camera over its lens disk (`lensSample(k)` times the radius) and shifts the projection by `-P00·lx/focus`, `-P11·ly/focus`, which holds points at the focus distance where they were; soft lights move over a disk perpendicular to their direction (`lightSample(k)`). The camera's position, quaternion and projection and the lights' positions are restored exactly after the draw, so nothing drifts between calls. With `reel check` it reports the un-jittered projection of labelled objects' world boxes (`project.ts`, clipped at the camera plane), `HtmlPlane` text through `HtmlLayer.reportThrough` (a point mapping in place of draw()'s affine transform), and a `stage` box carrying how many objects intersect the frustum.

## Readback and export

`readPixelsAsync` reads `finalRT` through a pixel-pack buffer and a fence (several times faster than a blocking `readPixels` in Chrome). The video path reads back in one of the `Pixels` modes the format asks for (`cli/formats.ts` plans it): `rgb8` (packed rgb24), `rgba8` (straight alpha), `rgb16`/`rgba16` (16 bits per channel, little-endian) or `linear` (the frame before post, half-float rgba, premultiplied). A packing pass per mode lays the frame's channels out as the bytes of ffmpeg's row stream, four bytes per texel of an RGBA8 target `PW * channels * bytes / 4` wide, so the copy is exact whatever the depth; `readStart(slot, mode)` queues it and `readFinish(slot)` collects it while the next frame renders. `stream()` sends frames over a WebSocket written from a worker thread, with at most `inflight` unacknowledged frames: the encoder's backpressure. `cli/video.ts` runs several pages (each its own Chrome and GPU process) on chunks of frames, puts the frames back in order, spools to disk when the encoder falls behind, and pipes them into one ffmpeg, whose arguments `ffmpegArgs` builds per format: full-range RGB to limited-range BT.709 YUV explicitly (chroma averaged over its block, accurate rounding), matrix, range, primaries and transfer tagged; PNG tagged sRGB; JPEG full-range BT.601 as its standard says; EXR marked premultiplied. An ffmpeg without libwebp writes PNG frames that `img2webp` assembles.

The page's `?bits=` (10 or 16, from the format) makes `finalRT` a float target and the final pass dither at that depth (1/1023 for 10-bit) instead of at 8 bits; `?alpha=1` is a transparent render (`ALPHA` in `format.ts`): `clearRT` turns an opaque clear to the theme's `bg` into transparent (as does `Stage3D` for a `bg` background), the stack of tracks and track 0's transitions start from transparent, the sub-frame sum carries alpha, and the final pass adds glow to the coverage, divides the premultiplied colour by it, runs the chain on straight colour, fades to transparent and writes straight alpha. Both are compile-time constants, so an 8-bit opaque page compiles the same shaders as before they existed.

HDR10 and HLG are not written. They would need: a PQ or HLG transfer in place of the sRGB encode and the shoulder (or a tone curve to a stated peak, 1000 nits for HDR10), BT.2020 primaries (the palette and textures converted from BT.709 into them), the 16-bit readback (already there) into 10-bit x265 or ProRes tagged `bt2020nc`/`smpte2084` or `arib-std-b67`, mastering-display and content-light metadata (`master-display`, `max-cll` in x265's parameters), and a way to judge the result: none of the preview, the stills or `reel check` can show HDR on an SDR display.

## Reporting and check

`report.ts` holds a module-level sink. `Engine.render` with `collectBoxes` opens it around the composite (around the sub-frame nearest t when there are several; paused during a stateful scene's pre-roll) and names each box with the drawing clip and its track, scaling its alpha by the clip's share of a crossfade and its track's opacity (so a clip fading out is not measured as if it were solid, and two clips of one track in a transition are not an overlap); outside that, `report()` and `reportText()` return on their first line. HTML layers report from the DOM in `draw()` (`domBoxes` in `html.ts`): each element's own text through a Range's client rects, computed size, colour and opacity, images, and overflow and clipping against the element's and its clipping ancestors' boxes and the layer's own edge (text that runs out of its own box counts as overflowing it rather than as clipped by the layer), all mapped through the draw transform. Each text box carries a `flow`, its nearest ancestor that is not `display: inline`, and `findingsOf` never counts two boxes of one flow as overlapping (a highlighted word inside its caption). `__reel.inspect(t)` prepares the frame, renders with collection, reads the pixels back and measures them with the pure functions of `src/check.ts` (a brightness grid per frame, each text box's text and background luminance); the CLI turns the samples into findings with `findingsOf` from the same module.

## Warm pages

The warm renderer (`cli/daemon.ts`, `cli/renderer.ts`) keeps pages open across requests. What that asks of the page: render errors are per request (`beginRequest`), a lost WebGL context is reported (`contextLost`) so the page is replaced, and nothing in the engine may depend on how many frames were rendered before (the same rule motion blur imposes on scenes).
